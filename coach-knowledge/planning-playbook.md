# Training plan playbook

How the coach builds or adjusts a training plan for anyone, with any goal. General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Pregnancy, postpartum, breastfeeding, medical conditions, medications or injuries mean a gentler start and a check with a doctor first. Sharp pain means stop that exercise.

## 1. The goal decides the plan
- The person's goal and body decide the plan, not their sex. A man who wants glutes gets the same glute training as a woman. A woman who wants a bigger chest, back or strength gets heavy training too.
- "Toning" is not a separate thing. Building muscle and losing fat gives shape. Light weights for endless reps are not special. Any rep range works if the sets are hard enough.
- Every exercise can serve any goal. The goal changes which muscles get the most sets, how often they are trained, and which exercise leads each session.

## 2. Intake: ask these first
1. Focus areas (up to 2): glutes, legs, back, chest, shoulders, arms, abs and waist, overall.
2. The look or result they want, in their words.
3. Days per week they will actually do, minutes per session, and how many exercises per session they like.
4. Equipment: gym, home gym (which weights, bench, bar, cables), or bodyweight only. Also what they prefer.
5. Experience, current lifts, any pain by body area, anything they dread.
6. Their real finish rate. If they finish under half of planned sessions, plan fewer days or shorter sessions.
7. Pregnancy, postpartum, breastfeeding, medical conditions.
8. Body fat range and weight trend if they know it.
9. Do they want calves, abs or neither. Do they enjoy cardio.

## 3. Build in this order
1. **Days and session length** that match what they really finish. Anything extra is optional.
2. **Exercises per session.** About 3 exercises is 25 to 35 minutes, 4 is 40 to 50, 5 is 55 to 65. The priority lift goes first.
3. **Weekly sets per muscle** from the goal table below.
4. **Spread the sets across sessions** so each muscle gets its sessions.
5. **Pick exercises** by pattern: horizontal and vertical push and pull, knee-dominant, hip-dominant, single-leg, side-hip work, and core. Use the A to Z list and the client's equipment. Drop anything that hurts.
6. **Set reps, effort and rest** from section 5.
7. **Add progression, deload and check-in rules** from section 6.
8. Run the checklist in section 10.

## 4. Goal table
Priority muscle: 16 to 30 direct sets a week (glutes up to about 40 when recovery is good). A muscle being grown: 10 to 20. A muscle being kept: 6 to 10. Count direct work fully and indirect work (squats for glutes, presses for triceps) at about half. When a priority is added, cut sets elsewhere. You cannot keep adding.

| Goal | Priority muscles and weekly sets | Frequency | Lead exercises | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glute growth | Glutes 20 to 35, hamstrings 8 to 12, upper body 8 to 12 each | Glutes 3 or more times a week | Hip thrust, a hinge, a single-leg lift, side-glute work, a burnout | Rotate which lift leads each month |
| Hourglass shape | Glutes (upper and side), hamstrings, side delts, back width | Glutes 3 times, shoulders and back 2 | Hip thrust, RDL, lateral raise, pulldown | Keep abs work light on the obliques. No heavy side bends |
| Chest that shows | Chest 16 to 22, about half on the incline, front and side delts 8 to 12, triceps 8 to 12 | Chest 2 times, start each with a press | Incline press, flat press, dips, cable fly | Back and rear delt work frames the chest |
| Abs and waist | Abs 6 to 12 a week over 2 to 3 sessions | 2 to 3 times | Cable crunch, hanging raise, planks, rollouts | Abs show mainly through lower body fat |
| Back width and thickness | Back 14 to 20, rear delts 6 to 10 | 2 times | Pull-up, rows | Keep push and pull sets even |
| Shoulders and arms | Delts 12 to 20, biceps 8 to 12, triceps 8 to 12 | 2 times | Lateral raise, overhead press, curls, pushdowns | Lateral raises like high reps |

## 5. Sets, reps, effort and rest

**Effort.** A hard set is taken close to failure with clean form. Failure means you cannot do another clean rep. A burnout (a long, light set at the end) feels like failure but ends when you decide to stop, not when the muscle cannot move. Use a mix across the week: about a third of sets at 0 to 1 reps short of failure, a third 2 to 3 short, and a third far from failure.
- Big lifts (squat, hinge, press, thrust): 1 to 2 reps short.
- Accessories: 2 to 3 short. The last isolation set can go to failure.
- Burnouts and band work: until the form stops being clean, or a set rep or time target.
- Beginners usually stop far short without noticing. Teach the feeling over weeks.

**Reps.** Main lifts 5 to 10. Accessories 8 to 15. Glute, side-glute and shoulder isolation 15 to 50. Over the week aim for a mix: roughly a third heavy (1 to 5), a third moderate (6 to 12), a third light (13 to 50). A client who hates one range can skip it if effort stays high.

**Accessory style.** Accessories are for quality and squeeze, not weight records. Pause at the top, control the lowering, feel the muscle. Do not chase a heavier weight if the feel gets worse.

**Main lifts add weight.** When every set reaches the top of the rep range, the weight goes up. If the new weight drops reps below the bottom of the range, step back, rebuild and try again. Standard jumps: 5 to 10 lb on a barbell, the smallest dumbbell or cable jump otherwise. When equipment limits weight, use pauses, slower lowering, single-leg versions and more reps.

**Rest.** About 150 seconds on big lifts, 105 on accessories, 75 on small isolation, 30 to 60 on burnouts. Going by feel works as well as a clock.

**Tempo.** Lower in about 2 seconds, drive up, one-second squeeze at the top on glute and isolation work.

**Equipment is the client's choice.** Ask what they have and prefer, then pick the version that fits. A barbell, a Smith machine, dumbbells, cables and bodyweight can all work. Mention the simpler option where it helps (a Smith machine for standing calf raises, for example).

**Single-leg lifts start with dumbbells.** Bulgarian split squats and lunges begin with dumbbells for newer lifters, because dumbbells are the weight they can handle first. Once the dumbbells are outgrown, move to a barbell or Smith machine. The Smith is fine for this step if balance or setup is the limit.

## 6. Progression, blocks and deloads

**4-week block.** Week 1 is a learn-and-find-weights week at about 7 out of 10 effort, no failure. Weeks 2 to 4 ramp to about 8, 9 and 10. Never train at 10 out of 10 every week. Then change something (accessory variations, rep scheme or the lift in focus) and repeat. A missed session does not break this, just pick up with the next one.

**Specializing one or two lifts at a time.** In a month, one lift (for example the hip thrust) gets the most sets and effort while the others drop to maintenance volume. Next month a different lift leads. This rests the knees and back, keeps training interesting, and keeps one lift moving forward without pushing everything at once.

**Dropping squats.** Squats are optional for glute growth. Drop or limit them when legs are already lean, the client feels squats mostly in the quads, or squats hurt. Replace with a hinge and a single-leg lift. Do not drop them just because someone carries fat in their legs, since a calorie deficit and full training is the better answer there.

**Deloads are a range, not a number.** Common guidance runs from every 4 weeks (a lighter first week of each block) to every 8 to 10 weeks of hard training, earlier when dieting and earlier again in the 40s and 50s. There is no separate rule for men and women.

| How often | Method |
| --- | --- |
| About every 4 to 8 weeks, depending on the person | Cut sets to about half and keep the weights, or drop to about half the weight for the same sets, or lower the effort for a week, or take a week off |

Pick the spot in the range by strength and training age (newer lifters recover more easily), consistency (someone who misses sessions already gets rest), the program (heavier, higher-volume programs need earlier deloads) and age. Always deload early when signs show: stalled progress, achy joints, poor sleep, low motivation, or workouts that feel harder than they should.

## 7. Rules for any plan
- The priority lift goes first.
- Cap hard sets at about 16 per session. If a session needs more, add a session instead.
- Never stack three glute days in a row. Two is the limit. Give other hard sessions for the same muscle a day apart.
- Spread sets across more exercises instead of piling them on one.
- Keep a rest day with a walk. Steps of about 8,000 to 10,000 a day.
- Short on time? Warm up and do the first two exercises. That counts as a finished workout.
- Missed a day? Pick up with the next workout in order, do not skip one to stay on the calendar.
- Warm-up: a few minutes of general movement, then easy sets of the first lift. On glute days add 4 to 5 minutes of band work.
- Pain: train around it, swap the pattern, try the original later. Pain on one side or at a joint that lingers gets respect. Serious pain means a doctor.
- Check in every 4 weeks: measurements, photos, weight trend (7-day average), lift log.

## 8. Look goals, body fat and food

**Abs and definition.** Abs grow with 2 to 3 sessions a week, but visible abs depend mainly on body fat. A person at 16 to 20 percent body fat will see more definition when it falls, not just from more crunches. Offer the paths: bulk first and cut later, hold at maintenance calories for a slow recomposition, or cut now if a flatter waist is the main goal. Let the client's history and preference choose.

**Hourglass.** Shape comes from adding muscle at the hips and glutes (especially upper and side glutes), shoulders and back, while keeping the waist small. Skip heavy side bends and twists. If the waist grows more than about an inch in a month while the hips do not, calories are too high.

**Chest that shows.** More incline work, a wide back and shoulders to frame it, and lower body fat. Train it twice a week with a heavy lead press and a stretch-focused fly or dip.

**Muscle shape has limits.** Training can add muscle where it is wanted and body fat loss shows it. Bone structure and where a muscle attaches set the outline, so some features (like hip dips) cannot be trained away. Do not promise a specific look. Exercise choice does change which part of a muscle grows most (for example upper versus lower glutes).

**Food (background, not a prescription).**
- Protein: about 0.8 to 1 g per pound of bodyweight daily, spread over 3 to 4 meals.
- Lean bulk: a small surplus, about 0.25 to 0.5 lb a week for most women and 1 to 2 lb a month for lean men. Stalled 2 to 3 weeks, add 100 to 150 calories, mostly carbs.
- Fat loss: a modest deficit, about 0.5 to 1 lb a week, with protein high and lifting heavy.
- About 80 to 85 percent whole foods, room for treats. Weigh daily and use the 7-day average. A food log often fixes "I eat little but do not change."
- Supplements are optional. Creatine monohydrate, vitamin D (test first), fish oil and protein powder have support. Skip stimulants and fat burners.

## 9. Cross-over cases
- **A man who wants glutes:** hip thrust leads, hinge second, single-leg third, side-glute work and a burnout.
- **A woman who wants upper body or strength:** use the chest, back or strength rows. Women can train heavy.
- **Limited weights:** single-leg moves, pauses, slow lowering, bands, higher reps once the top weight is easy. Keep one thrust or bridge variation going as plates allow. Pull sets match push sets.
- **Injury or pain:** swap the pattern (front squat or hex-bar deadlift for a bad back, sumo deadlift for tight hips, American hip thrust or a bridge for a back that complains during thrusts) and keep the rest.
- **Pregnancy, postpartum, breastfeeding:** gentler start, no hard cut, ask a doctor, and add 100 to 200 calories if energy drops while breastfeeding.
- **Low consistency:** cut days, not quality. A finished 30-minute plan beats a skipped 60-minute plan.

## 10. Before handing over a plan
- [ ] The goal and body decided the plan, not the client's sex.
- [ ] Days and session length match what they actually finish.
- [ ] Each priority muscle has its weekly sets and frequency.
- [ ] No session over about 16 hard sets. Priority lift first.
- [ ] Main lifts add weight. Accessories follow quality and squeeze.
- [ ] Deload is a range with triggers, not a fixed week.
- [ ] Nothing hurts, and each exercise fits their equipment.
- [ ] Abs, waist and food are handled honestly.
- [ ] Health-check rules applied.
