# Recommendation guide

If-then advice for when a client gives feedback ("my quads burn on thrusts," "I want more hamstrings," "I only have dumbbells"). Find the situation, offer the option, and say it comes from training experience, not a promise. General fitness guidance, not medical advice.

## Glutes
- **Where growth comes from.** Roughly 85 percent from hip extension work (thrusts, bridges, hinges) and about 15 percent from squats and lunges (an expert estimate). "Just squat" is weak glute advice, but squats and lunges still add the stretched position and lower-glute work.
- **Side-glute work is about 15 percent of glute gains.** It mainly builds the upper glutes (the shelf). Hard to load, so use high reps (15 to 50) and burnouts. If a plan is heavy on abduction, trim it before trimming thrusts or hinges.
- **Barbell bridges usually go heavier than thrusts.** Keep thrusts as the priority and use heavy bridges now and then.
- **Frog pumps and bridges** are low-quad, high-rep options, 30 to 100 reps.
- **Low-soreness volume.** Quadruped hip extensions and kickbacks add glute work with little soreness or recovery cost. Good for 3 x 20 mid-workout, or when someone is tired or short on recovery.
- **Some people cannot fire their glutes with straight legs.** About one in four new clients. Try: short plank from the knees, then a plank with tight glutes, then a standing glute squeeze, then a pelvic-tuck thrust hold or pulse. Use as homework, a warm-up, or a burnout finisher.
- **Full-depth squats** grew glutes and inner thighs more than half squats in a study of beginners, with lighter weight. Squat as deep as form allows.
- **Goblet squats** can activate the glutes as much as heavy squats and are a good bridge to barbell squats.
- **Thrusts help squats.** In small studies, thrust training raised squat strength without squatting. Likely, not proven.
- **Squats are optional** for glute growth. Drop them if legs are lean, they feel them mostly in the quads, or they hurt. Do not drop them just because someone carries fat in their legs.
- **Hip dips are mostly bone.** Do not promise to train them away. Talk about what training changes (muscle and body fat).

## Hip thrust problems
| Client says | Try |
| --- | --- |
| Feels it in the quads | Slide feet away from the body, lower the bench, band above the knees, push through the heels |
| Feels it in the hamstrings | Slide feet closer, taller bench, push through the whole foot |
| Feels it in the low back | American hip thrust, ribs down, move from the sternum down, tuck the chin, lower the weight |
| Slides on the bench | Align the shoulder blades with the bench edge, secure the bench against a wall |
| Bench is too tall (about 5'1" or shorter) | Bench near 12 inches, or an aerobic step with risers |

## Hamstrings and legs
- **Thrusts and squats barely grow the hamstrings.** Add knee-flexion work (leg curls, sliding curls, Nordic curls, glute ham raise) for the lower hamstring, plus hinges for the upper hamstring.
- **Quads much stronger than hamstrings raises injury risk.** Give hamstrings equal attention.
- **Lunge differences.** Walking lunges cause the most soreness. Step-ups cause much less and suit beginners. Reverse lunges are easier on the knees. Bulgarian split squats: do one leg, rest, then the other, because alternating weakens the stretched leg. Start with the weaker side.
- **Single-leg lifts can replace squats** for people whose squats hurt, since most people can lunge nearly as much as they squat.
- **Calves:** bottom-half partial reps grow calves as well as full range. Standing raises grow the deep calf muscle nearly as well as seated. Calves respond to higher reps twice a week.

## Upper body and shape
- **Rear and side delts are often the lagging parts.** Add rear delt or face pull work and lateral raises with high reps.
- **Bodyweight training:** progress with harder versions, more reps and slower lowering. More reps with a fixed load grows muscle about as well as adding weight. At home, keep pull sets at least equal to push sets.
- **Chest definition for women** does well with upper-chest work (incline, feet-elevated push-ups).
- **Slim waist goal:** skip heavy side bends and twists. Crunches, dead bugs and planks work abs without widening the waist.
- **Abs** grow like other muscles, 2 to 3 sessions a week, add weight when bodyweight is easy (about 30 to 50 clean reps). Visibility depends on body fat.

## Training and recovery
- **Effort over volume.** More sets at low effort stall people. Raise effort or load before adding sets.
- **Soreness is not the goal.** No soreness does not mean no growth, and too much soreness hurts frequency.
- **Strength goals keep people coming back** more than looks goals. If gains are slow, make a lift target the goal.
- **Bodies vary.** Muscle size varies up to about 5 times between people before training, and response to a block varies a lot. Never say "bad genetics" after one block.
- **Cuts do not need a new program.** Keep the same rep ranges while cutting.
- **40s and 50s:** train just as hard, recover more slowly, deload earlier if run down.
- **Water and the scale:** weight can stall while fat drops. Ignore the scale in the week before and during a period. Track waist.

## Equipment feedback
- **Only light dumbbells (about 25 lb):** single-leg moves, pauses, slow lowering, bands, higher reps. Move to a barbell or Smith machine when the dumbbells are outgrown.
- **No gear at all:** the bodyweight ladders in the A to Z list. Pulling needs a bar or sturdy table.
- **Smith machine:** fine, and often simpler for calf raises and split squats. A barbell for squats, hinges, thrusts and presses if the client has and prefers one.
- **Machines:** a thrust bench or any stable bench setup works for thrusts. Machines are never required.
